# The independent parts of the development, and how to compile them separately

Two Lean modules of this repository are **dependent** when one imports the other,
directly or transitively. The connected components of that relation are the
*independent parts* of the project: no module of one part imports a module of
another, so the parts can be compiled separately — in different jobs, different
checkouts, or concurrently on one machine — and a change inside one part cannot
invalidate any other.

Each multi-module part has its own Lake target in `lakefile.toml`, whose `roots`
are the maximal modules of the part (those nothing else imports). Building the
target builds exactly that part:

```
lake build BookProofRealification        # 22 modules (BookProof/Realification/)
lake build BookProofInducedSystems       # 20 modules, disjoint from the above
lake build BookProof.ChapterBosonicCCR   # a part that is a single module
```

None of the part targets is a default target; `lake build BookProof` still
builds the whole development, and `lake build` the four default targets
(`BookProof`, `Book`, `Singularity`, `Layout`).

The inventory below is generated by

```
python3 scripts/import_components.py BookProof            # the report
python3 scripts/import_components.py BookProof --lakefile # the Lake stanzas
python3 scripts/import_components.py --all                # every library
python3 scripts/import_components.py BookProof --check    # verify the targets
python3 scripts/import_components.py --all --markdown     # this inventory
```

`--check` verifies that every multi-module part has its target, that the target's
`roots` are the maximal modules of the part, and that the transitive import cone
of those roots is exactly the part (it currently passes for every target; `python3 scripts/import_components.py BookProof --write-lakefile` regenerates the stanzas in place).

## Sub-system targets

Some coherent pieces of the development are far too connected to the rest to be
independent parts of their own — their dependencies are shared with a part — yet
they are exactly what one wants to compile while working on them. `lakefile.toml`
therefore also carries **sub-system targets**: the `roots` are the modules the
sub-system is *about*, and its cone (the transitive imports those roots need) is
by construction a subset of the containing part's cone. Such a target can never
duplicate work across jobs; it only lets one job compile less than the whole
part. The concrete ones are listed under *Sub-system targets of `BookProof`* at
the end of the inventory.

`--check` verifies them as well: the roots must be real modules, they must be
exactly the maximal modules of the cone they generate, and the cone must stay
inside a single part — a cone spanning two parts would redo work that another
job is already doing.

Re-run it after adding modules: if a new module imports two previously
independent parts it merges them, and the script says so by reporting fewer
parts (and an `UNNAMED component` on stderr when a new multi-module part has no
target name yet — add it to `COMPONENT_NAMES` in the script and regenerate the
stanzas).

Keeping the parts apart is a design rule, not only a measurement: when a new
module only needs results of one part, import from that part alone. For example
the lattice-translation example of the gauge chapter
(`BookProof/Qym/ChapterGaugeShiftExample.lean`) is a separate module from
`BookProof/Qym/ChapterGaugeIncompleteFixing.lean` precisely so that the gauge
foundations do not acquire a dependency on the operator-theoretic core.

Every other first-party library (`Book`, `GapCertificate`, `Audits`, `Work` and
the Riemann-route libraries) is a separate Lake library; `Audits` and `Work` are
never default targets.

### Sub-system targets of `BookProof`

A sub-system target is a *view* of one part: a coherent piece of the development that is far too connected to be a part of its own. Its `roots` are the modules it is about, and its cone is a subset of the containing part's cone, so it never duplicates work across jobs -- it only lets one job compile less.

| Lake target | roots | modules in the cone | inside the part |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `BookProofDerivativeGauge` | `BookProof.Ns.ChapterNsBrstDerivativeGauge`, `BookProof.Ns.ChapterNsFieldMomentumInverse`, `BookProof.Qg.ChapterQgBrstDerivativeGauge` | 106 | `BookProofOperatorCore` |

Unlike a part target, a sub-system target is not checked for closure: its cone is strictly larger than the sub-system, by construction. `--check` verifies instead that its roots are real modules, that they are exactly the maximal modules of the cone they generate, and that the cone stays inside a single part.
