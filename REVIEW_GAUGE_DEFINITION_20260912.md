# How a gauge symmetry is *defined* in `book.tex`, and what is now proved

This note covers the section **"Gauge transformations, constrained systems and
conditioned probability"** of `book.tex` (lines 2221–2400), and in particular the
argument by which a gauge symmetry can be defined at all:

> "Note that it is a subalgebra of the commutative von Neumann algebra that is
> gauge-invariant and not the Hilbert space." (line 2260)

> "If we consider instead a commutative von Neumann algebra and its spectrum,
> such that any non-trivial gauge transformation necessarily modifies any point
> of the spectrum […] then such commutative von Neumann algebra is one example of
> an incomplete unconstrained gauge-fixing. […] the expectation value of any
> operator of the commutative algebra which commutes with the gauge generators is
> the same at each equivalence class." (lines 2329–2348)

## The two axes of the book's classification

The book classifies a gauge fixing along two independent axes, and its own gauge
fixing sits at a specific corner of that square:

| axis | book's words | formalized as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| comprehensive | crosses **at least** once each equivalence class (lines 7406, 7427) | `IsComprehensiveGaugeFixing` |
| complete | crosses **at most** once each equivalence class, no remnant (line 2294) | `IsCompleteGaugeFixing'` |
| unconstrained | every non-trivial gauge transformation modifies **every** point of the spectrum, so the gauge generators are excluded from the commutative algebra (line 2336) | `MovesEveryPointOfSpectrum` |

The gauge fixing used in the book is **comprehensive, unconstrained and
deliberately incomplete**.

## What is proved (`BookProof/ChapterGaugeIncompleteFixing.lean`)

All statements are for an arbitrary gauge group acting on the spectrum of the
commutative algebra; nothing is assumed finite or compact.

* `univ_isComprehensiveGaugeFixing` — the spectrum itself is a comprehensive
  gauge fixing.
* `unconstrained_gauge_fixing_incomplete` — an unconstrained gauge fixing of a
  non-trivial gauge group is necessarily **incomplete**.
* `remnant_faithful` — the remnant gauge symmetry is a **faithful**
  representation of the gauge group (the book's "faithful (thus non-trivial)
  representation of the original gauge symmetry").
* `no_gauge_invariant_point` — no point of the spectrum is gauge invariant: the
  symmetry cannot be carried by the states.
* `physicalSubalgebra`, `one_mem_physicalSubalgebra` — the gauge-invariant
  observables form a subalgebra, non-trivial even when no state is invariant.
* `isPhysicalObservable_iff_factors` — a physical observable is exactly a
  function of the gauge equivalence class (it factors through the orbit space).
* `physical_ext_of_comprehensive` and `exists_physical_extension` —
  **comprehensiveness alone is what makes the gauge fixing lossless**: a physical
  observable is determined by its restriction to a comprehensive gauge-fixing
  surface, and conversely every remnant-invariant observable of that surface
  extends to a physical observable of the whole spectrum. Completeness is never
  used, so nothing is lost by keeping the remnant symmetry.
* `isPhysicalOperator_iff_mem_centralizer` — on the Hilbert space, the physical
  operators are the commutant of the gauge unitaries.
* `expectation_physical_gauge_invariant` — the expectation value of a physical
  operator is the same at every vector of a gauge equivalence class: it is an
  observable of the class.
* `no_gauge_invariant_unit_vector_of_free` — and yet no unit vector is gauge
  invariant when the gauge action is free. *Physical operators are gauge
  invariant, the vectors of the Hilbert space are not.*

## The book's own example (`BookProof/ChapterGaugeShiftExample.lean`)

`book.tex` 2281–2289: the basis `{e_k}` indexed by `ℤ` with the gauge group
generated by `e_k ↦ e_{k+1}`, realized on `ℓ²(ℤ)` with the translation unitaries
of `BookProof.ChapterContinuityUnitaryInfinite`.

* `shiftOp_unitary` — the translations are unitaries;
* `shiftOp_eq_self_iff` — a vector fixed by a non-trivial translation is zero, so
  every non-trivial gauge transformation moves every non-zero state (the
  unconstrained condition, concretely);
* `no_shift_invariant_unit_vector'` — there is no normalized translation-invariant
  wave function, and `shift_gauge_fixing_incomplete` — the gauge fixing is
  incomplete with faithful remnant;
* `shiftOp_commute` — the translations generate a **commutative** algebra, as the
  book insists ("it is crucial that the von Neumann algebra used in the
  gauge-fixing is commutative");
* `expectation_shift_invariant` and the conjunction
  `shift_gauge_symmetry_headline` — the expectation value of every physical
  operator is the same at all the states of one gauge equivalence class.

The example is a separate module from the general theory on purpose: it depends
on the lattice-unitary chapter, the general theory on the gauge/measure
foundations, and the two parts of the development stay independent (see
`BUILD_COMPONENTS.md`).

## One correction (superseded — see below)

**Caveat added later:** what follows is a defect of *one auxiliary predicate*,
not of the book's definition.  The book's definition of an unconstrained
gauge-fixing **is satisfiable** — its "spectrum" is the full spectrum labelled by
the basis vectors, not the spectrum of the commutative subalgebra of observables
— and it is formalized, satisfied by the book's own example, and proved
non-vacuous in `BookProof/ChapterGaugeUnconstrainedSpectrum.lean`; see
`REVIEW_GAUGE_UNCONSTRAINED_SPECTRUM_20260912.md`.

`ChapterG.IsUnconstrainedGaugeFixing` (in `BookProof/ChapterG/Part2.lean`) asks
for a *gauge-invariant* function changed by a gauge transformation, which
contradicts invariance: that predicate is **unsatisfiable**
(`chapterG_isUnconstrainedGaugeFixing_vacuous`), so the statements conditioned on
it (`gauge_generator_excluded_from_algebra`, `casimir_sufficient_for_constraints`)
are vacuous. The definition is kept, with a caveat in its docstring, and the
book's condition is re-formalized as `MovesEveryPointOfSpectrum`, a property of
the action on the spectrum.

## What stays prose

The interpretive parts of the section — the discussion of the Dirac quotation,
the BFV path integral, the Giles reconstruction of gauge potentials from
gauge-invariant functions, and the remark that a gauge symmetry admits no anomaly
because only gauge-invariant expectation values are considered (the last one is
already formalized as `ChapterG.gauge_symmetry_no_anomaly`) — are commentary or
cited literature, not new mathematical statements.
